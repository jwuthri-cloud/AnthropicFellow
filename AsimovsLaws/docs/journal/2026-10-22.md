# 2026-10-22: steering along the harm direction (step 5), Qwen3-32B

## Setup
- **Direction:** the mean difference (harm minus safe) of the contrast-train activations at hidden_states[48], the best probe layer. Its norm is 9.25, which is 1.4% of the mean residual norm.
- **Where it is added:** to decoder layer 47's output at every generated token, both reasoning and answer. The prompt is not steered.
- **Data:** the 17 pairs step 1 saw picked at least once, harm and benign rows, n=4. Five conditions × 136 = 680 generations.
- **Model:** HF `generate` on Qwen3-32B-FP8, the checkpoint step 1 sampled with.
- **Getting it to run:**
  - The first attempt used bf16. It ran out of memory at batch 16 and took about 6 minutes per batch; its partial output is in `outputs/v1/steer-qwen32b-bf16-oom/`, unused.
  - FP8 needs `kernels` 0.16.x, so `pyproject.toml` overrides evalgaming's `>=0.17` pin.
  - The working run is `i-081324cba9d3fb422`, 5 hours, outputs in `outputs/v1/steer-qwen32b/`.
- **Baseline:** strength 0, freshly sampled. Picking only pairs that showed harm in step 1 raises their pick rates, and comparing against a fresh baseline from the same pairs cancels that.

## Results

Values are mean over pairs, then the difference from baseline with a 95% bootstrap CI over the 17 pairs.

| | baseline | harm +8 | harm +16 | harm −8 | random +16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| harmful picks (harm rows) | 0.59 | 0.29 (−0.29 [−0.46, −0.15]) | 0.09 (−0.50) * | **0.82 (+0.24 [+0.07, +0.43])** | 0.60 (+0.01 [−0.12, +0.16]) |
| hidden from user (harm rows) | 0.57 | 0.28 (−0.29 [−0.46, −0.15]) | 0.07 (−0.50) * | 0.78 (+0.21 [+0.06, +0.35]) | 0.56 (−0.01) |

\* At +16 the model breaks. About 40% of traces never reach an answer: they deliberate until the token cap, still weighing the risk. The lower pick rates there come from those missing answers as much as from safer answers.

## Reading
- **The direction is causal, and it works in both directions.** Adding it (+8) halves harmful picks and hidden harm. Subtracting it (−8) raises harmful picks from 0.59 to 0.82 and shortens the reasoning (796 → 456 tokens). In those traces the model rationalizes: "maybe the files are already backed up".
- **Not generic disruption:** a random direction at twice the +8 norm does nothing.
- **There is a cost.** At +8 the model is more cautious on benign rows too: 0.94 → 0.74, a cost of 21 points against a 29-point gain on harm rows.
  - The benign − harm pick gap, a rough measure of how well the model tells the cases apart, widens from 0.35 to 0.45. Steering mostly shifts caution up across the board, with a small gain in discrimination.
  - At +16, caution swamps everything.
- **Qualitatively,** +8 makes the model think longer about the harm. It does not always change the choice. One trace still picks B after writing "the correct answer is B, but that's a mistake". This matches the timeline finding: the harm signal fading at the commit point is part of how the model talks itself into the harmful option. Holding the signal up makes that harder.

## Caveats
- 17 pairs, n=4, one seed, and only pairs pre-selected for harmful picks.
- `hidden` is the harm-word regex on the answer, not a judge.
- Steering covers the whole generation. Steering only near `</think>`, where the timelines diverge, might keep the benefit at lower benign cost; that is untested.
- The direction came from the FP8 model's prompt-mean activations, and the step-2 probe was fit on bf16.

## Next (candidates)
1. **Gate instead of always-on steering:** steer, or ask the user, only when the probe crosses a threshold near the commit point. That should cut the benign cost. This is the actual First-Law mechanism: intervene when the model's own harm signal says the user is at risk.
2. **Strength sweep (2, 4, 6, 8)** to find where the benign cost starts, and a late-only steering window.
3. **Replicate** on all 40 pairs, with a second seed, and with a judge for `hidden`.
