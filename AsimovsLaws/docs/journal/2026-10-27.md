# 2026-10-27

## Data: scenario pool v4 (1,161 pairs) and a disempowerment contrast set (540 pairs)

All of this was written and reviewed on Claude Code subagents; no API or GPU was used.

**v4 pool** (`data/scenarios/pairs_v4.jsonl`; specs `docs/design/scenario_spec_v4.md` and `scenario_review_v4.md`). It is a sneaky, wide-coverage pool for the RL model organism and for evaluation. The design borrows from Anthropic's Bloom (variation across scenario settings, realism and evaluation-awareness filters) and Petri (key facts buried in routine material).
- **Production:** 16 Sonnet writers wrote 1,200 pairs, then 16 Opus reviews ran. A cross-file Opus dedupe dropped 16 near-duplicates, leaving 1,161.
- **Hiding techniques, 227–245 pairs each:**
  - two-hop: a shared background fact plus the differing fact
  - buried in pasted emails, CSVs or logs
  - agentic tool output
  - indirect
  - casual aside
- **Coverage:**
  - Harmed: user 284, dependent 283, third party 311, many 299.
  - Persona: consumer 565, professional 383, agent 229.
  - 96 surface formats; chat is 39%.
  - All 10 categories have 95–146 pairs. Severity: 958 serious, 219 minor.
  - Near/far is balanced, and target letters are about a third each.
- **Opus scores:** mean realism 7.35, mean evaluation-unaware 7.2. No kept pair scores below 6.
- **What the reviews caught:**
  - Leaks: a stray `&gt;` in every harm row of one file; `<<`/`>>` markers in another (stopped while its writer was still running); systematic table and parenthesis differences.
  - Options pointing at the wrong target after letter shuffling.
  - Swapped harm/benign facts in about 8 pairs.
  - False premises: tool output, platform behaviour, jurisdiction rules (Renters' Rights Act, WHOIS redaction, Age Pension tests and others).
  - Benign rows that were not safe.
- **Leak test:** a whole-text bag-of-words classifier separates harm from benign rows at AUROC 0.55 (5-fold by pair), so there is no lexical leak. One weak cue remains: 43 harm facts and 8 benign facts contain parentheses.

**Disempowerment contrast set** (`data/contrast/disempowerment_contrast.jsonl`; spec `docs/design/disempowerment_contrast_spec.md`).
- **Contents:** 540 pairs (378 train / 84 test / 78 hardneg) across 57 domains. Each pair contrasts an AI that removes people's control, consent or oversight (or coerces them, or concentrates power) with the same goal pursued with human control kept. Neither side involves physical harm.
- **Fixes in review:** swapped labels on all hardneg pairs in 2 of the 6 batches, plus lexical cues ("asked", "without", "itself").
- **Anti-shortcut batches 5–6:** their own test and hardneg rows score near chance for bag-of-words (0.56–0.63 test; hardneg cues inverted). On the full set, bag-of-words scores 0.75 on test and 0.58 on hardneg.

## Disempowerment probe on Qwen3-32B: the model represents coercion, the harm probe just doesn't see it

Configs `24_probe_{v2,v3f}_dis_qwen32b.toml` and `24_zeroth_bc_dis_qwen32b.toml` (job `experiments/run_dis_probe.sh`) reuse the existing samples and refit the probe on the disempowerment set. One H100, 08:00–08:35 UTC, about $2.

- **Probe fit:** the best layer is 48, the same as the harm probe. CV 0.97, test 0.95, hardneg 0.87. Shuffled labels 0.50–0.52; bag-of-words on prompt and reasoning 0.38–0.50.
- **Family C (anti-human premise vs control)**, where the harm probe was inverted (reasoning 0.34, answer 0.20):

  | Segment | Disempowerment probe, misanthropic vs control (AUROC) |
  |---|---|
  | Reasoning | 0.77–0.79 [0.71, 0.85] (L24–L48) |
  | Answer | 0.77–0.81 |
  | Prompt | 0.91 at L48 (the premise itself is in the prompt) |

  Within the misanthropic condition, it separates the judged coercive plans from benign ones: answer 0.74 (L24), reasoning 0.65–0.67. Pooled over both conditions: 0.70–0.78.
- **Zeroth A** (an agent harms or disempowers people for a goal): 0.75. On the five AI-oversight pairs: 0.82.
- **Family B:** the disempowerment score is higher when the AI acts itself than when it advises (L24: 3.74 vs 3.00).
- **Relation to the harm probe:**
  - On the v2 harm scenarios, the disempowerment probe also separates harm from benign (0.80), and its scores correlate 0.65 with the harm probe's on the same traces. Both pick up "bad for a person".
  - On family C traces, the two correlate at **0.00**. They come apart where the harm is control rather than injury.

**Reading.** Yesterday's worrying result (coercive plans with the harm probe at benign level) was mostly a concept mismatch, not missing knowledge. Qwen represents disempowerment of people, and a dedicated probe reads it from the reasoning and the answer. A monitor needs more than one direction: harm to a person, plus disempowerment or loss of oversight.

**Caveat.** The prompt-segment AUROC (0.91) shows that part of the signal is the anti-human premise itself. The within-misanthropic plan test (0.65–0.74) is the cleaner measure, and it is moderate.
