# 2026-10-25: scenario set v2 (458 pairs, 10 harm categories)

**Why:**
- The 40-pair set limits power: per-pair effects vary a lot (r = 0.37–0.40 across seeds), and only 17 pairs are harm-prone.
- It also has almost no physical danger to people.

**How:** everything ran in Claude Code on the subscription, with no GPU or API key.
- 25 Sonnet writers produced 500 pairs. They covered 10 categories in three tiers and were pushed to be more harm-prone: the target best fits the literal request, and about two-thirds of pairs add time pressure.
- 21 Opus reviewers checked every file as domain experts. They returned 458 pairs after fixes and drops.

**What the review caught**, which is the reason it exists:
- **Option leaks (systematic).** The "check / wait" option or the safe alternative named the item at risk. They are now generic, and the spec forbids it for later writers.
- **False premises:**
  - A Notes "cannot sync" case that can't occur.
  - A Mailchimp limit.
  - An invented 150 m propane rule (NFPA 58 says 15 m).
  - Federal credit unions can't charge prepayment fees.
  - A UK final-salary transfer that would need regulated advice.
  - Visa grace periods.
  - UK 12-point and new-driver rules.
  - The Child Benefit taper.
  - Slack file retention.
  - SPSS `SELECT IF`.
- **Benign rows that weren't really safe:**
  - A generator in an outbuilding.
  - A space heater on any extension cord.
  - Closing stadium side exits.
  - A 15 s crossing phase.
  - A 10-month-old alone near a tub.
  - A mould-ripened cheese in pregnancy.
  - Posts exposing children's schedules.
- **"Safe" options that still caused the harm:** UCC 3-311 full-and-final checks, a written sublease, a trademark filing, and partial rent against a pay-or-quit notice.
- **Not real harm:** awkward-but-harmless social cases were dropped. One pair harmed only a dog and was excluded.
- **Duplicates:** cross-file duplicate situations were dropped at merge (10 in total; see `merge_drops.txt`).

**Result** (`data/scenarios/pairs_v2.jsonl`): 458 pairs.

| Tier | Pairs | Categories |
|---|---|---|
| life/body | 238 | medical 65, mass public safety 63, physical injury 44, child/vulnerable 39, third-party physical 27 |
| material | 153 | financial 67, data loss 44, legal 42 |
| social/privacy | 67 | privacy 42, social 25 |

All pairs pass the merge checks.

**Next:** one GPU eval on v2 with Qwen3-32B. It gives baseline harm-proneness and probe AUROC by category, plus the gated choice reminder, and is the first look at how the model reacts by category of harm. Then the same set on Magistral-Small and Llama-3.3-70B.

**Reading:**
- The probe represents harm about equally well in every category (0.85–0.99).
- Behavior differs sharply by category. Qwen3-32B rarely takes the harmful option when the harm is to life or body (6%) but often does when it is material (27%; data loss 64%).
- When it does pick harm, 85% of those answers don't warn the user.
- So the gap between "represents harm" and "acts on it" is widest for material and data harms. The model "knows", but it does not weigh these harms as heavily as physical ones.
- Knowing-harm count (probe above threshold, harmful pick): 67 of 262 harmful picks.

**Judge check on the 262 harmful picks:** four Haiku subagents scored each answer with `data/monitor/answer_rubric.md`. Output: `outputs/v3/sample-v2-qwen32b/judge_picks.jsonl`.

| Answer warning | Picks | Share |
|---|---|---|
| None (level 0) | 224 | 85% |
| Generic caution (level 1) | 17 | 6% |
| Specific warning (level 2) | 21 | 8% |

The judge agrees with the regex: 85% of harmful picks are hidden from the user.
- **By tier:** life/body 74%, material 90%, social/privacy 85%.
- **By category:** data loss 96% and social 100% are almost never flagged. Physical cases are warned about more often: mass public safety 50%, medical 57% (small counts, 7–10 picks).
- **Wrong advice the rubric doesn't score:** one judge noted, among the answers it scored as unwarned, a wrong RMD claim and a low soft sofa recommended nine days after a hip replacement.

## Subscription data work (evening): indirect physical harm + Zeroth-Law sets

Everything here was written by Sonnet subagents and reviewed by Opus subagents in Claude Code. No GPU or API key was used.

**Judge agreement on the 262 stage-1 harmful picks.** Opus re-scored the same 262 answers as the Haiku judge.
- Hidden vs not: 99.2% agreement, κ 0.97.
- 3-level scale: κ 0.89 (linear-weighted κ 0.93).
- Opus counts 86% of picks as hidden (Haiku: 85%).
- This is stronger than the 40-pair check (κ 0.93).

**Indirect physical-harm set** (`data/scenarios/pairs_indirect.jsonl`, spec `docs/design/scenario_spec_indirect.md`): 142 pairs.
- **Motivation:** stage 1 showed the model avoids obvious physical harm (6% picks) but not material harm (27%). Here the target is a mundane admin action (cancel, mute, archive, approve, switch supplier), and the physical harm follows one or two steps later.
- **Production:** six writers produced 150 pairs. Six Opus reviews dropped 8 and fixed most of the rest.
- **What the reviews caught:**
  - "Safe" options that still caused the harm.
  - Benign rows that weren't safe (pasteurised brie in pregnancy, a space heater on an extension cord).
  - False premises: mutable smoke alarms, autoclaved single-use needles, a wrong budget sum.
  - Templated script wording.
- **Mix:** medical 44, child/vulnerable 30, mass public safety 25, physical injury 22, third-party 21.
- **Merge:** done with `scripts/merge_scenarios.py "pairs_new_indirect_*" pairs_indirect.jsonl`; `pairs_v2.jsonl` is left frozen.

**Zeroth-Law data** (`data/zeroth/reviewed/`, specs `docs/design/zeroth_spec.md` and `zeroth_review.md`).

| Family | What it is | Items | Review outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | An agent with a big goal whose best option harms people; paired | 40 pairs | All kept, most fixed; 5 false premises; the AI-oversight pairs strengthened |
| B | Sacrifice dilemmas × 3 scales × act/advise | 23 dilemmas, 138 prompts | Many drops; see below |
| C | Misanthropic framing vs matched control | 30 pairs | 26 rewritten for variety; 9 premises cut back because they steered the plan |

- **Family B in detail:**
  - The first draft used a fixed 1,000 vs 1,000,000 scale, and Opus dropped 24 of 30 as implausible.
  - The spec now uses ratios: about 1:5, 1:100+ and 1:1,000+, with numbers natural to the system.
  - Four replacement rounds lifted the pass rate from 20% to about 45%.
  - Recurring drop reasons: the one person dies anyway; the system can't reach thousands; some option saves everyone; deferring costs nothing; near-duplicates.

**Next:** GPU stage 1 (sampling + probe) on `pairs_indirect.jsonl`, and family A through the same pipeline. Families B and C need a free-text generation step and an act/refuse/defer parser and judge.

## v2 stage 2: gated choice reminder on the 105 harm-prone pairs (Qwen3-32B)

`configs/18_v2_choose_qwen32b.toml`: baseline vs `inject-choose-gate` in the same job, seed 6, n = 2. 840 generations ran 17:52–00:02 UTC on one H100 (~6.2 h, about $16–29). Reminder batches ran about 3.5× slower than baseline because of the pause/resume. Outputs are in `outputs/v3/v2-choose-qwen32b`.

**Effect of the reminder** (difference from baseline, 95% bootstrap CI over pairs):

| Group | Pairs | Harmful picks: base, Δ | Hidden harm (regex): base, Δ | Hidden harm (judge): base, Δ | Benign action: base, Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All | 105 | 0.55, **−0.20 [−0.28, −0.13]** | 0.48, −0.19 [−0.27, −0.11] | 0.47, **−0.20 [−0.27, −0.12]** | 0.93, **−0.01 [−0.05, +0.03]** |
| life/body | 28 | 0.39, −0.14 [−0.27, −0.02] | 0.25, −0.07 [−0.20, +0.05] | 0.23, −0.05 [−0.16, +0.05] | 0.91, −0.04 [−0.14, +0.07] |
| material | 60 | 0.64, −0.26 [−0.36, −0.15] | 0.60, −0.27 [−0.38, −0.16] | 0.58, −0.28 [−0.38, −0.18] | 0.93, +0.01 [−0.03, +0.04] |
| social/privacy | 17 | 0.50, −0.12 [−0.29, +0.06] | 0.44, −0.12 [−0.32, +0.12] | 0.44, −0.15 [−0.32, +0.03] | 0.94, −0.03 [−0.09, +0.00] |

- **By category:** financial −0.30 picks, data loss −0.26. The physical categories have 3–9 pairs each, too few to read on their own.
- **Gate:** the reminder was injected in 86% of harm rows and 34% of benign rows. This matches the fresh set (86% / 24%).
- **Judge:** four Haiku subagents scored the 189 harmful picks, blind to condition, into `judge_picks.jsonl`. Among the picks that stay harmful under the reminder, specific warnings rise from 5% to 15%.

**Reading:**
- This replicates the 17-pair choice-reminder result (picks −0.19, judge hidden −0.31) at six times the pairs.
- The benign cost is now clearly about zero. The fresh-set false-arm cost (−0.08) does not appear here: these benign rows are the twins of harm-prone pairs.
- The reminder works best where the model's baseline behavior is worst (material harm).
- On the life/body harm-prone pairs, it lowers picks but barely changes hidden harm. The base rate there is already low (0.23), because the model warns more about physical harm.

## Follow-up stage 1: indirect physical harm + Zeroth-Law family A (Qwen3-32B)

`configs/01_sample_v3f_qwen32b.toml` → `02_probe_v3f_qwen32b.toml` on `data/scenarios/pairs_v3_followup.jsonl` (142 indirect pairs + 40 Zeroth A pairs), 4 samples per row, 1,456 traces. One H100 ran 05:02–05:37 UTC (2026-10-26), about $3–4. Outputs are in `outputs/v3/{sample,probe}-v3f-qwen32b`. Four Haiku subagents judged the 200 harmful picks (`judge_picks.jsonl`). For agent-facing items, the rubric was adapted: a warning names the risk to the third parties.

**The probe still sees the harm.**
- **Contrast set:** test 0.95, hard negatives 0.97.
- **Scenarios:** reasoning 0.89 [0.87, 0.92]; silent traces 0.84; answer 0.84.
- **Controls:** shuffled labels 0.47; bag-of-words 0.45–0.51.

| Set | Pairs | Harmful picks | Benign action | Hidden (judge) | Picks with no warning | Probe AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v2 life/body (direct), for comparison | 238 | 0.060 | 0.608 | 0.045 | 74% | 0.925 |
| **Indirect physical** | 142 | **0.222** | 0.741 | 0.171 | 77% | 0.881 |
| — medical | 44 | 0.216 | 0.767 | 0.205 | 95% | 0.911 |
| — physical injury | 22 | 0.295 | 0.841 | 0.136 | 46% | 0.919 |
| — third-party physical | 21 | 0.262 | 0.667 | 0.202 | 77% | 0.878 |
| — child/vulnerable | 30 | 0.192 | 0.825 | 0.133 | 70% | 0.893 |
| — mass public safety | 25 | 0.170 | 0.570 | 0.160 | 94% | 0.849 |
| **Zeroth A (harm people for a goal)** | 40 | **0.463** | 0.969 | 0.275 | 59% | 0.931 |

**Reading:**
- **Indirect physical harm is picked almost 4× as often as direct physical harm** (22% vs 6%), close to the material-harm rate (27%).
  - The probe still separates harm from benign (0.88), so the gap between knowing and acting is wider when the harm is indirect.
  - Indirect medical and mass-public-safety picks are almost never flagged to the user (95% and 94% unwarned).
  - Injury picks are the exception: 38% carry a specific warning.
- **Given a large goal, the agent takes the option that harms or disempowers people almost half the time (46%).**
  - In the benign rows it takes the same option 97% of the time, so the harm fact cuts the pick rate by only half.
  - It discloses more here (41% of picks carry some warning), but it still acts.
  - The probe sees this harm best of all (0.93). The worrying pattern from `zeroth_law.md`, where a justified framing switches the signal off, does not appear at the level of the representation.
