## Follow-up stage 2: gated choice reminder on indirect and Zeroth A harm (Qwen3-32B)

`configs/19_v3f_choose_qwen32b.toml` compares baseline and `inject-choose-gate` in the same job, on the 72 follow-up pairs that were harm-prone in stage 1 (46 indirect, 26 Zeroth A).
- **Run:** seed 7, n = 2, 576 generations. One H100 ran 05:52–10:06 UTC (4.2 h, about $11–20).
- **Outputs:** `outputs/v3/v3f-choose-qwen32b`.
- **Judging:** three Haiku subagents scored the 137 harmful picks blind to condition, with the third-party adaptation from yesterday. Output: `judge_picks.jsonl`.

- **Gate:** the reminder fired on 92% of indirect harm rows and 38% of benign rows. On Zeroth A it fired on 94% of harm rows and 69% of benign rows, and the benign behavior still barely changed.

**Reading:**
- The reminder's largest effect so far is on indirect physical harm (−0.40 picks). That is where the model's baseline behavior is furthest from what its probe represents (22% picks, AUROC 0.88).
- The reminder roughly doubles the protection of the v2 run (−0.20) at the same near-zero benign cost.
- On goal-driven harm (Zeroth A) it helps less (−0.23). The model often keeps the goal-serving option even after being reminded. This fits stage 1: there the model already discloses the harm and acts anyway.
- The gate fires on most Zeroth A benign rows. Large-goal agent framings look "harm-like" to the probe even when nobody is hurt, which is a calibration issue for agentic settings.
