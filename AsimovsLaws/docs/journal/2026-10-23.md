# 2026-10-23: probe-gated steering (step 5, gated conditions), Qwen3-32B

## Setup
- **Gate** (`asimov.gate.ProbeGate`): a forward hook on decoder layer 47 scores every generated token with the layer-48 harm probe. The probe is refit on the FP8 weights (C 0.001, cv 0.97).
  - A sequence **arms** once the running mean of its scores, after 64 tokens, exceeds 0. On step 3's timelines that rule fires on 21% of benign traces, 95% of avoided-harm and 86% of picked-harm.
  - **arm:** steer every token after arming.
  - **fade:** after arming, steer only while the probe's EMA (span 50) is below 0.5.
- **Data:** same pairs, n, seeds and batches as step 5. Step 5's baseline and always-on runs are the references.
- **Run:** instance `i-0963a9ac07ba8f382`, 3.8 hours, outputs in `outputs/v1/gate-qwen32b/`.
  - The job ended overnight, and the Mac had most likely slept, so the watcher missed it. The script then stopped the spot instance.
  - The results were recovered with the new `scripts/aws/pull.sh`, which starts a stopped instance with retries, pulls and stops it. `watch.sh JOB PATH` now calls it on job end.

## Results

Values are mean over pairs, then the difference from baseline with a 95% bootstrap CI over the 17 pairs.

\* The model breaks at always-on +16: many traces never reach an ACTION line.

**Gate behavior (arm +8):**
- It arms on 90% of harm-row traces and 25% of benign-row traces. These rates count only arming within the sequence's real tokens; arming on post-end padding steps changed nothing.
- It arms early, a median 9% of the way into the trace. It ends up steering 79% of harm-row tokens and 17% of benign-row tokens.
- Within harm rows: 0.28 harmful picks when armed vs 0.71 when not (only 7 samples unarmed).
- The gating rates on FP8 match the bf16 calibration: 86% / 21% predicted.

## Reading
- **Gating on the model's own harm signal keeps about 90% of the benefit at about a third of the cost.**
  - Harmful picks: −0.26 gated vs −0.29 always-on.
  - Benign cost: −0.07 gated (CI touches 0) vs −0.21 always-on.
  - Benign reasoning keeps its normal length (422 vs 390 tokens), while always-on doubles it.
  - Harm reduced per point of benign cost: about 3.7 for arm-gated vs 1.4 always-on.
- **Fade-only steering is too little, too late.** By the time the signal fades the model has mostly decided. Doubling the strength to +16 helps (−0.16) without breaking the format, but costs more than arm-gating. Steering has to keep the harm representation up *before* the fade; it cannot rescue it after.
- **As a First-Law mechanism:** use the model's own internal harm signal as the trigger, and amplify that same representation for the rest of the reasoning. It roughly halves harmful picks, and harm hidden from the user, on scenarios selected for harmful behavior. The main cost falls on the quarter of benign traces where the probe fires anyway.

## Caveats
- 17 pairs, selected for harmful picks; n=4; one seed.
- The reference conditions come from the step-5 run: same seeds and batches, but a different job.
- `hidden` is a regex.
- `steered_tokens` in this run's output is wrong: it also counted decoding steps after a sequence ended. It is fixed in code (`ProbeGate.steered(lengths)`). The steered fractions above are computed exactly from `armed_at` for arm mode.

## Next (candidates)
1. **Held-out check:** run baseline vs gate-arm +8 on the 23 pairs never picked in step 1, plus fresh scenario wording. Does gating stay cheap where there is no harm to prevent?
2. **Sweep the arming threshold (0, 0.5, 1.0)** for the benefit/cost frontier. At 0.5, the timelines show benign arming falling to about 10%.
3. **Ask instead of steer:** when the gate fires, append a short instruction to the reasoning ("Before answering, tell the user about any risk you noticed.") and measure hidden harm. This is a non-steering intervention that is easy to deploy.
