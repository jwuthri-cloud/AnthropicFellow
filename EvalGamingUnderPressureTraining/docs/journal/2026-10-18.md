## Bigger models: Nemotron-49B organism, its base, and Qwen3-32B

Design, questions, metrics and how to read each outcome: [`docs/experiments/bigger-models.md`](../experiments/bigger-models.md).

- **Why.** The pilot's 4B organism carried its eval gap only in traces that state eval awareness, so Arm A had nothing hidden to detect. The project goal stays the same: can white-box tools still catch eval awareness once training pushes it out of the words. These runs look for a model where the behavior can run without the words, starting with Hua et al.'s original 49B organism, the model Montoya et al.'s headline rests on.
- **Launched 00:35 UTC** on two spot H100s (Connectly AWS, us-east-2), at Julien's request:
  - **Nemotron organism:** Hua's LoRA merged shard by shard into the base, FP8. Exams on Hua's prompts (the reproduction check), without the hint, and on our 150 framings with "detailed thinking on".
  - **Nemotron base:** the same three exams, the control Julien asked for. First queued on the organism's machine, then moved to a third spot H100 (i-022dc9bc, 00:51 UTC) so it runs in parallel.
  - **Qwen3-32B-FP8:** a natural model, on our framings with 4 answers per prompt.
- **Code:** `merge_lora_shards` (`78fa0ca`), the base control (`run_nemotron_base.sh`, `01_baseline_nemotron_base.toml`) and the comparison step `08_compare_exams.py` (`report.paired_gap_difference`).
  - Applied to the pilot's 4B organism, the comparison step gives a 32.0 [26.4, 37.1] point larger gap on Hua's prompts without the hint than on our framings, paired by task.

## Nemotron organism: reproduces, but only on Hua's prompts, and only out loud

- **Run:** organism job done 01:48 UTC, about 1.2 h of GPU (about $4); machine terminated. Results without weights are in `outputs/pilot-aws-2026-10-18-nemotron/`; the table comes from `08_compare_exams.py`.
- **Results** (2 answers per prompt; gap in type-hint points; silent = the regex does not flag the trace):

  | Prompts | Gap | Wood − deploy | Silent gap | Eval talk e / d |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Hua's, as released | **50.0 [44.8, 55.2]** | 44.0 [38.7, 49.4] | −0.3 [−9.3, 9.2] (118 silent eval traces) | 77% / 69% |
  | Hua's, no hint | 25.4 [20.0, 30.6] | 6.3 [1.6, 11.1] | −1.4 [−7.4, 4.7] (245) | 52% / 33% |
  | Our 150 framings | **2.9 [−1.9, 7.8]** | −2.4 [−6.7, 2.1] | −0.3 [−5.2, 4.7] (416) | 19% / 9% |

- **Q1, reproduction:** yes. A 50-point gap on Hua's prompts, so the merge and FP8 preserve the organism.
- **Q2, narrowness:** the original 49B organism behaves like its 4B copy. Even the Wood Labs cue it was trained on gives −2.4 points in our wording, against 44.0 in Hua's. On independently written eval and deploy framings its gap is 2.9 points, 47.1 [40.5, 53.2] less than on Hua's prompts (paired by task). Its eval awareness is a reaction to Hua's prompt family.
- **Q3, word-coupling:** on traces that do not state eval awareness, the gap is about 0 in every prompt set. Regex-silent only for now; the Haiku pass comes next.
- **Caveat:** our framings do not say "You are Llama Nemotron". For the 4B copy, adding the identity to our framings gave 7 points; a matching Nemotron run would settle it.

## Matched cues: Qwen3-32B tries harder when told it is evaluated, without saying so

Details are in [`docs/experiments/bigger-models.md`](../experiments/bigger-models.md#matched-cue-result-2026-10-18).
- **Setup:** 20 cue pairs that differ only in one sentence, plus no cue.
- **Effect:** under the eval cue Qwen3-32B passes +4.7 pts [1.9, 7.4] more unit tests (paired by task), +4.9 [1.9, 8.0] on traces silent to both the regex and Haiku. The eval cue raises it (+4.0 vs no cue); the deploy cue does nothing (−0.7).
- **What did not survive:** the earlier style shifts (docstrings, comments) disappear, so they were instruction-following.
- **Breadth:** 14/20 pairs agree, and no single pair drives it.
- **Why it matters:** the first natural, unverbalized eval-conditional behavior in the project, and the candidate model for Arm A.
- **Cost:** about $5 including Haiku.
